The journal

A tasting journal worth keeping.

Cigar Locker gives each cigar you enjoy a page in a tasting journal: a rating out of ten stars, the flavors you noticed in each third, your notes, and two photos.

Try Cigar Locker on the web

Free to try, with nothing to install: it runs in your browser and you sign in with Apple. It is laid out for a computer’s screen first. For adults 21 and over.

  • Ten starsAnd, if you like, a score each for construction, draw and burn.
  • Flavors by the thirdSixteen to choose from, for the first, middle and last third.
  • Private unless you shareFriends see an entry only when you tell them.

An entry takes a few taps

  1. 1
    Pick the cigar from your humidor. Its count goes down by one.
  2. 2
    Rate it out of ten stars. If you like, also score its construction, draw and burn, each out of ten.
  3. 3
    Tap the flavors you noticed in the first, middle and last third, from a list of sixteen: cocoa, coffee, cedar, leather, earth, pepper and the rest.
  4. 4
    Add tasting notes and two photos if you like: the cigar and the moment.

Why thirds, and which flavors

A cigar changes as it burns. The first inch is rarely what the last one is, and one note for the whole cigar hides that. So the journal asks what you noticed in the first third, the middle and the last, from sixteen flavors that cover most of what people report:

Cocoa, coffee, cedar, leather, earth, pepper, baking spice, cream, nuts, caramel, toast, oak, citrus, floral, hay and dried fruit.

The list is short on purpose. A fixed list means two entries a year apart use the same words, so they can be compared, and it takes a tap where typing would take a minute. Anything the list lacks goes in your notes.

How your likely rating is worked out

For a cigar you have not rated, Cigar Locker can show a likely rating. It comes only from your own ratings, never from anyone else’s.

  • It finds the cigars you have rated that are most like the new one, by wrapper, strength, filler, binder, country and size, and weighs the closest most heavily.
  • It needs at least three rated cigars before it says anything.
  • It is shown to the nearest half, as “about 8.5”, with the cigar it is closest to and what the two share.
  • It is always marked as an estimate. It is a guess at your own taste, not a score for the cigar.

A cigar that is not in your humidor

A cigar from a shop, or one someone handed you, can go straight into the journal. Scan it or type it in, say whether you bought it or it was a gift, and write the entry. It never passes through a humidor.

The day and the time

Each entry keeps its date, and the time of day if you want it. An entry can be edited later, or undone, which puts the cigar back.

What the journal gives back

  • Every entry for a cigar is on that cigar’s page, so you can see how it changed with age.
  • Your ratings shape your likely rating for cigars you have not tried, and what Suggest One picks from your humidors.
  • The whole journal can be exported to a spreadsheet, or to a PDF with your photos.

Private unless you share

A journal entry is yours. Friends see one only when you decide to post it, and you choose which friends. Nothing is posted publicly.

Questions about the journal

How are cigars rated in Cigar Locker?

Out of ten stars, in whole stars, by you. Cigar Locker shows no critic’s score and no average of other members’ ratings.

Can I record flavors as well as a rating?

Yes. Each entry can note the flavors of the first, middle and last third from a list of sixteen, with free tasting notes beside them.

Can I journal a cigar I do not own?

Yes. A cigar from a shop, or one given to you, can be entered straight into the journal without being added to a humidor.

Is my journal public?

No. Entries are private. A friend sees an entry only if you choose to share it with them.

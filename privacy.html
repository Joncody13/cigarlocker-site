Privacy policy

Your privacy

Effective November 10, 2026

This policy explains what information the Cigar Locker app ("Cigar Locker", "the app"), Cigar Locker on the web and this website collect, why, who it is shared with, how long it is kept, and the choices you have. Cigar Locker is provided by Atlantis Dev, LLC ("we", "us"), P.O. Box 31, Haverhill, MA 01831. It is made for people in the United States, and our server is there. Questions about this policy go to support@cigarlocker.app.

The short version

  • Your collection lives on your phone first. If you sign in, it is backed up to our server in the United States.
  • We record how you use the app, and the errors it runs into, linked to your account, to understand, fix and improve Cigar Locker.
  • We may send emails about your account, news, and emails from Cigar Locker partners, which you can turn off. We choose which partner emails to send you using your collection and activity; that choosing happens inside Cigar Locker, and partners never receive who you are.
  • Photos you scan are sent to our server and to our AI provider, Anthropic, to identify the cigar. Our server doesn't keep the scan photo; it keeps what was read from it.
  • Humidity sensors of our own are planned. Today the app shows sample sensors with pretend readings, which never leave your phone.
  • What you share in Friends is seen only by the friends it is for, and by our moderators if it is reported.
  • You can delete your account, and what our server holds about you, inside the app. Section 16 lists the little that stays.
  • Cigar Locker is only for adults 21 and over.

1. What stays on your phone

Cigar Locker keeps your collection in a database on your iPhone, so it opens instantly and works without a connection. That includes your humidors, shops, cigars, purchases (what you paid, your own value for them if you set one, where, when, how they arrived and why you bought them, if you say), what has happened to each cigar, your journal and your photos. Without an account, your collection stays on your phone; the app still sends the usage and error records described in section 12, under an identifier for the app on your phone. The features that use our server (sections 5, 6, 7 and 9) need an account.

Some things never leave your phone at all: the sample humidity sensors and their pretend readings (section 8), and the app's settings, such as your chosen style and colors.

Cigar Locker on the web (cigarlocker.app/app) works the same way in your browser while you are signed in: it brings your backup into the browser's own storage, photos included, and sends back what you change. When you sign out there, the browser forgets your collection and photos. Signing in on the web uses Sign in with Apple, as the app does.

Importing a collection reads the spreadsheet file on your phone; the file is not sent to us. Exporting creates a file on your phone that goes only where you send it.

An Android version is planned. This policy will be updated when it arrives.

2. Your account

You sign in with Apple. Apple tells us an account identifier and an email address. If you choose Apple's Hide My Email, that address is a private relay address Apple creates, and we never see your own. We ask Apple only for your email, not your name. We use the account to back up your collection, to let you use the features that run on our server, and to email you.

We may send three kinds of email:

  • Account emails, about your account, your plan and its price, security, and changes to our terms or this policy. These may come for as long as you have an account.
  • News from Cigar Locker: new features and tips.
  • Emails from Cigar Locker partners: news and offers from shops, makers and other partners, sent by us. We do not give partners your email address, or anything else about you.

News and partner emails start switched on when you create your account. Turn either off at Settings > Account or with the unsubscribe link in any of them. We record whether an email was delivered and opened, and which of its links you tapped. A link in one of our emails goes first through cigarlocker.app, which records the tap with your account, and then on to the shop's website with a different random code made for that one tap (section 13 says what a shop can tell us with it). We use this to see which emails work.

We choose who gets which news and partner emails using what we know of you: for example your age, where you live, what is in your collection, what you buy and enjoy, and how you use Cigar Locker. The choosing happens inside Cigar Locker and we send the email ourselves, so a partner never receives who you are. A partner email may go to members near a shop or to members anywhere, and some are sent automatically when your collection changes. We may be paid a share of what you spend through a link or code in one, and each such email says so. We do not send offers to members who live in a place we know forbids tobacco coupons, or who have not told us where they live. Section 11 says more.

3. Your profile

Before your account is used, we ask for your date of birth, to confirm you are 21 or older. The account goes no further for anyone younger (section 18). We also keep your market insights choice and your email choices, and when you last changed them.

You may also give your ZIP code, your name, phone number and home address. These are optional. The ZIP code is used for the weather where you live (section 7) and, with the rest, to choose which news and partner emails suit where you live, for market insights if you allow them (section 11), to run your account and to contact you.

4. Your backup

When you are signed in, your collection is backed up to our server: your humidors (with the humidity range you set for each), shops, cigars, purchases (with what you paid and any value of your own you set), what has happened to each cigar, and your journal entries (ratings, flavors, tasting notes and dates). Photos you take or choose for a cigar, a purchase, a humidor or a journal entry are stored in private storage under your account. Product photos from other websites are not copied; only their web address is kept.

Our server also keeps the earlier versions of these records (for example, how many of a cigar you had before and after each change), and builds from them a record of each purchase and of the shops you buy from. Something you delete in the app is marked as deleted in your backup rather than erased, and its earlier versions and photos stay on our server, until you delete your account.

We use your backup to restore your collection on a new phone and to keep your phone and the web in step, to run and improve Cigar Locker, to understand how members collect, and, with your email choices, to choose which news and partner emails to send you. Other members cannot see it. People who work for Atlantis Dev, LLC can view account information, including backups and Friends content, when that is needed to run the service, keep it safe or meet the law.

5. Scanning a band or box

When you scan, the photo is sent to our server and on to Anthropic, our AI provider, whose models identify the cigar and mark the points used to measure it. A list of the kinds of cigar you have (their names and sizes) may go with it, so that a photo of a shelf, a row or several boxes can be matched to what you own; filming a row or choosing a video sends still pictures taken from it the same way. Some scans also search the web for the band or compare it with product photos on makers' and shops' websites; those searches use what was read from your photo, and those websites never receive your photo.

  • Our server does not store the scan photo. It keeps what was read (the cigar's name, size, measuring points and a description of the band) with your account for about a day, so your phone can collect the answer if the app was closed mid-scan. Our server's logs record the same reading, without the photo or your name, for up to 30 days.
  • On your phone, the photo becomes the purchase's photo if you save it, and is then backed up with your collection like any other photo you take.
  • The photo you keep shows the cigar without the bill, card, coin or ruler it was measured by. If the saved photo still shows one (for example, it was saved before the app could cut it out), the app sends that photo to our server and Anthropic once more to find the cigar and cut the rest away; the same happens when you choose Remove the Bill or Card for a photo. That photo is not stored on our server either.
  • When you save a scanned cigar, a description of its band in words (its colors, shape and emblem) may be added to a shared band library that helps everyone's scans recognize that cigar. That description is not linked to you.

6. Look-ups and suggestions

These features send information to our server, which asks Anthropic when it doesn't already know the answer:

FeatureWhat is sentWhat is kept
Cigar details (looked up after a scan or a search of the catalog, "Look up its details", and, in the background, collecting facts already known for your cigars) The cigar's brand, line, size and wrapper. The facts found (blend, factory, sizes, the maker's suggested price, availability) are kept in a shared library for everyone, not linked to you.
Shop look-up The shop name and town you type, with your humidors' names and places as a hint to the right branch. A shop you've saved without a photo or logo is looked up once on its own. The shops found (name, address, photo, logo) are kept for 90 days as a shared look-up, not linked to you.
Pairings The cigar's details; or, from a drink or dish, the cigars in the humidor you choose, with their details. Drinks and food for a cigar are kept in a shared library, not linked to you.
You may also like A cigar you rated highly, with your rating, and the other cigars in your humidors, with their details. Nothing on our server. The answer is kept on your phone.
Suggest One, right now The cigars in the humidor you choose (their details, how many are left, your ratings, and how many days since each was suggested or enjoyed), your choices (inside or out, length, strength, meal), your local day and time, and your ZIP code if outside. Nothing beyond the weather look-up in section 7.
Suggest One, a trip The same, with the place and dates you type. The place you typed and its location, as a shared look-up, not linked to you.
Places (cigar shops and lounges) The town or ZIP code you type; or, only when you tap Near me, your phone's location at that moment. Your recommendations, "been here" marks, what you say a place is known for, and any problem you report. Your location is used to find the nearest town and the places around you, and is not kept. A town's search and the places found are kept in a shared directory for everyone, not linked to you. Your recommendations, marks and reports are kept with your account; other members see only how many members recommend a place or have been there.

We use what is sent to answer you and, where the table says so, to build shared libraries that are not linked to you. Anthropic processes it on our behalf as our service provider (section 14).

7. Weather and places

For the weather, our server finds where your ZIP code, or the place you typed for a trip, is from its own table of US ZIP codes; only for one that table lacks does it ask Zippopotam.us. It then asks the US National Weather Service for the forecast there. Those services receive the ZIP code or place from our server, never your name, email or account. A place's location is remembered on our server, and its forecast for a short while, so they need not be asked for again; they are not linked to you.

For Places, our server searches the web for a town's cigar shops and lounges and finds where each one is from its street address through the US Census Bureau's geocoder. They receive the town and the places' addresses, never anything about you. The map in Places is Apple Maps, which your iPhone loads from Apple; your iPhone may also ask Apple where a listed place's street address is.

A few places that Cigar Locker itself chose are listed first in their area, each marked "Cigar Locker's pick". Nobody pays for the place.

8. Humidity sensors

Humidity sensors of Cigar Locker's own are planned, and this policy will be updated before they arrive. Today the app shows sample sensors: pretend sensors with pretend readings, made on your phone so you can see how it works. They are not part of your backup, and their readings never leave your phone. While the sample is on and you are signed in, the app tells our server when it last refreshed the sample and whether that worked, with the app's version; we keep one such note for your account, to find faults.

9. Friends

Friends is optional. If you join, we keep:

  • your username, the name you choose to show beside it (optional), a photo of yourself if you add one, whether people may find you by search, and when you agreed to the friends' rules;
  • invite codes you make, friend requests, your friends, the people you have blocked, and the friends you have hidden your posts from;
  • your posts (the cigar, your note and any photo you add), who each post is for, your replies, and your messages, including posts and replies you share in a message;
  • reports you make, with what they are about.

Nothing is public. Your posts and their photos are seen only by the friends you choose, and your own photo only by your friends. A reply you write under a friend's post is seen, with your username, by the friends that post is for. Among members, messages are seen only by you and the friend you write to. A friend you've hidden your posts from does not see them and is not told. People can find you by search only if you allow it, except by typing your exact username. Someone you have blocked can't see you or contact you. When you search for people, what you type is used to find matches and is not kept.

To keep Friends safe, an automatic filter keeps certain words (slurs, threats and the like) out of posts, replies, messages, usernames and names. When something is reported, our moderators review it within 24 hours. A moderator sees the report (who made it and the reason given) and what was reported: the post, reply or message itself, a reported post's photo, or a reported member's username, name and photo. The report does not show a moderator the rest of the conversation. They may leave it up, take it down, suspend its author (who can then no longer post, reply or send messages, and whose posts and replies are hidden from other members meanwhile), or remove the author's account altogether. The person reported is not told who reported them.

A post, reply or message that you remove, or that a moderator takes down, is deleted from our server for good 30 days later, unless a report about it is still open. Reports are kept while they are needed to keep Friends safe.

10. Images from other websites

For a cigar or shop without a photo of your own, the app may show a product photo, shop photo or logo from another website, such as a maker's or a shop's, or a site icon from Google's site-icon service. To show it, your phone loads it from that website, which receives the ordinary information any web request carries, such as your IP address and device type. We don't control those websites; their own privacy policies apply. "Open in Maps" on a shop's or a place's page hands its address to Apple Maps.

11. Market research and partner emails

When your profile's market insights box is ticked (it starts ticked), your collection (what you buy, where, what you pay and why, what you rate and enjoy) and your ZIP code, if you gave one, may be used to understand collectors and the cigar market, to improve Cigar Locker and what it suggests, and to choose the content and partners' features we show in the app. Insights drawn from it may be shared with, or sold to, makers, shops and other partners in the cigar trade, in a form that does not identify you.

  • We do not give partners your name, email or username as part of these insights.
  • You can change your choice any time at Settings > Account.

Emails from Cigar Locker partners

With Emails from Cigar Locker partners on (Settings > Account, on from the start), we use your collection and activity in Cigar Locker (what you keep and how fast it goes, what you buy, where and what you pay, what you rate, search for and look at, and the likely ratings the app has shown you) to choose which occasional partner emails we send you. The choosing happens inside Cigar Locker. Shops, makers and other partners never receive your name, email, collection or activity to choose you; we send the email ourselves. We keep when you made the choice and the words you made it under.

With it off, we send you no partner emails, and your collection and activity are not used to choose any.

12. Allowances, usage and error records

To keep the service fair and its costs in check, our server counts how many scans, look-ups, suggestions and shop look-ups each account uses each day and each month, and what is left of the welcome allowance, and notes when each was used. Like any online service, our server also keeps short technical logs of requests (such as the time, the feature used and whether it worked) to keep it running and to find faults.

If you subscribe to Cigar Locker Plus, Apple handles the payment: we never see your card or billing address. Apple tells our server the subscription's status (active, in a grace period, expired, revoked or refunded), its dates, the product and an identifier of the transaction, and we keep those to know whether your account has Plus. We may ask Apple about it again from time to time, and Apple sends our server a notice when it renews, is cancelled or is refunded.

We also record how you use the app, linked to your account (or, before you sign in, to a random identifier for the app on your phone, which is joined to your account when you sign in):

  • when you open it and how long it stays open, with the app's version, the kind of device (iPhone or web) and your time zone;
  • what you search for in your collection and which cigar you open after, which cigars' pages you look at, and the likely ratings the app shows you;
  • scans: when one starts, finishes or fails, how long it took, what it named, and, when you correct it, what it said and what you saved in its place. Never the photo, and never your notes;
  • first steps and features used, such as confirming your age, making an account, your first humidor and cigars, an import, a journal entry, Suggest One, a pairing, a trip, a shop or friend added, and looking at or subscribing to Cigar Locker Plus;
  • when a link carrying campaign tags opened the app, that campaign's source, medium and name, so we know which of our own emails, posts or pages brought people to Cigar Locker.

We use all of this to run and improve Cigar Locker, to see how members use it, and, with Emails from Cigar Locker partners on, to choose the partner emails we send you.

Error reports. When something goes wrong in the app, it sends our server a report: what failed and where in our code, with email addresses, web address details and long numbers stripped out, the app's version and build, your phone's model and its iOS version, and your account when you are signed in. Reports never include your photos, notes or passwords. We use them only to find and fix faults, and delete them after 90 days. Apple's own crash reports reach us only if you have chosen, in your iPhone's settings, to share them with app developers.

13. Advertising, tracking and sharing

  • Today Cigar Locker shows no advertising and has no advertising or analytics companies' code in it.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and we do not give your information to data brokers.
  • We do not share any one person's likes, purchases or contact details with shops or makers unless that person chooses to.
  • A link in one of our emails goes through cigarlocker.app, which records that you tapped it, and then on to the shop's website with a random code made for that one tap. The code tells the shop which email and link the visit came from, not who you are. The shop, or its affiliate network, may tell us that a visit with that code added something to a cart or placed an order, and the order's total; we count that for the email and the link, and do not add it to what we know about you.
  • If any of this changes, we will update this policy before it does and, where the law or Apple requires it, ask you first.
  • We do not read your phone's contacts. Cigar Locker uses your phone's location only when you tap Near me in Places, and only while you are using the app; it finds the places near you and is not kept. Once you have allowed it, the map also shows where you are, drawn on your phone by Apple Maps. Otherwise the only location we know is the ZIP code or place you type. The camera and your photo library are used only for the photos you take or choose. While you film a row of cigars, the app reads your phone's motion to tell you when to slow down; that stays on your phone.

14. Who we share with

We share information only with the companies that run parts of Cigar Locker for us, each under its own terms with us, and as the law requires:

  • Supabase hosts our server, database, private photo storage and sign-in, in the United States.
  • Anthropic provides the AI models that read scans, look up cigar, shop and place facts, and make suggestions and pairings. It receives what sections 5 and 6 describe, under its commercial terms, which do not allow it to train its models on what we send.
  • Apple provides Sign in with Apple, delivers the app, takes payment for Cigar Locker Plus, and draws the map of shops and lounges (Apple Maps, which receives the area shown and, if you allow it, your location to show where you are).
  • Expo delivers app updates to your phone. Its update service receives a random identifier for the app on your phone and ordinary request information.
  • Resend is the email service we use when we send email. It receives your email address and the email, and tells us whether it was delivered and opened and which links were tapped.
  • GitHub hosts this website and the files of Cigar Locker on the web (GitHub Pages), and may keep standard logs of visits.

Some public services answer our server's questions without learning anything about you: the US National Weather Service and Zippopotam.us receive a ZIP code or place for the weather, and the US Census Bureau's geocoder receives the street addresses of shops and lounges (section 7). Google receives ordinary request information when this website loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, and when the app shows a site icon from Google's site-icon service (section 10).

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, to protect members or the public from harm, or as part of a sale or reorganization of our business, in which case this policy continues to apply to it.

15. How long we keep it

  • Your account, profile and backup, with its photos, earlier versions and the records built from it: until you delete your account. Today we do not delete an account for going unused.
  • Friends: your username, friends, posts, replies and messages until you remove them or delete your account. Something removed is deleted for good 30 days later, unless a report about it is still open.
  • A scan's reading: about a day.
  • Usage counts, the usage records in section 12, your choices about emails and market insights, and the record of emails we sent you: until you delete your account. Usage records from an app that never signs in are not linked to anyone and are kept while useful.
  • Error reports: 90 days.
  • Server logs: up to 30 days.
  • Reports made about content in Friends: while they are needed to keep Friends safe.
  • An email address that bounced, or from which our email was marked as spam: kept on a list of addresses we do not email.
  • Shared libraries (cigar facts, band descriptions, pairings, shop and place look-ups) and counts of what an email led to are not linked to anyone and are kept while useful.

16. Deleting your account

Go to Settings > Account > Delete account in the app. This permanently removes from our server:

  • your account (the identifier and email address Apple gave us) and your profile;
  • your backup, its earlier versions and all its photos, with the purchase records and everything worked out from them for choosing partner emails;
  • your username, your photo, friends, friend requests, invite codes, blocks, posts, replies and their photos, and the messages you sent and received;
  • the reports you made, and your recommendations, marks and reports in Places;
  • your scan readings, usage counts and usage records, your error reports, the record of emails we sent you, and our record of your Cigar Locker Plus subscription.

What stays: reports other members made about your content, with your account removed from them, to keep Friends safe; an email address on our do-not-email list (section 15); and the shared libraries, which are not linked to you. Server logs clear within 30 days, and copies in our hosting provider's backups are overwritten within 30 days.

Your collection stays on your iPhone until you delete the app; on the web, the browser forgets it. Deleting your account does not cancel a Cigar Locker Plus subscription: cancel it in your Apple ID's subscriptions, or Apple keeps charging for it. To take Cigar Locker off the list of apps that use Sign in with Apple, use your Apple ID's settings.

17. Your choices and rights

  • See and take your data: export your collection and journal as spreadsheet files at Settings > Your data.
  • Correct it: edit anything in the app; your profile at Settings > Account.
  • Delete it: delete entries in the app (section 4 says what our server still keeps of them), or your whole account (section 16).
  • Emails and market research: change them any time at Settings > Account (sections 2 and 11).
  • Friends: choose who sees each post, hide your posts from anyone, turn off being found by search, block anyone.

Depending on where you live, you may have further rights, such as to ask for a copy of what we hold about you or to opt out of certain uses. Write to support@cigarlocker.app; we will answer within the time the law sets and won't treat you differently for asking.

18. Age

Cigar Locker is only for adults 21 and over. The app asks you to confirm you are 21 or older when it is first opened, and asks for a date of birth when an account is made. Signing in gives us the identifier and email address from Apple before the date of birth is asked; for anyone younger than 21 the account goes no further: nothing is backed up and the features on our server stay closed. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 21; if we learn we have, we delete the account and its information. If you believe someone under 21 is using Cigar Locker, write to support@cigarlocker.app.

19. Security

Information travels between the app and our server encrypted (HTTPS). On our server, each account's backup, photos and Friends content are protected by access rules so that, among members, only that account, or the friends it chooses, can read them. On your phone, your sign-in is kept in the secure Keychain; on the web, in your browser's own storage until you sign out. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affects your information, we will tell you as the law requires.

20. This website

This website sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. It loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, so Google receives your browser's ordinary request information. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may keep standard logs of visits. The sign-in page loads Apple's sign-in from Apple. Links in our emails pass through this website on their way to a shop (section 13).

21. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version here with a new effective date. Before a change that matters to how we use your information, we will tell you in the app or by email, and ask you first where the law or Apple requires it.

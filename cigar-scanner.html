Locker Vision

Identify a cigar from a photo.

How a scan works

  1. Photograph it. A band, a box or a whole shelf, with the camera or from your photos.
  2. Cigar Locker identifies it. It reads a band the way a collector does: its colors, shape and emblem as well as its words, set beside the bands in its band library. It names the cigar, then fills in the blend and the sizes the line comes in.
  3. You confirm, then save. Check the size and wrapper with a tap, or correct them. Say how many and which humidor. Nothing is saved until you do.

Getting a good read

Most scans that fail do so for the same few reasons, and each has a simple fix.

  • Fill the picture with the band. A band photographed from across a table is a few dozen pixels wide, too small to read. Hold the phone a hand’s width away.
  • Turn the lettering toward the camera, in even light. Glare on cellophane hides print: tilt the cigar a little, or slide it out of the cellophane.
  • Get every band in. Many lines share a main band and differ only in a second band or a ribbon at the foot. If your cigar has one, it is often what tells two lines apart.
  • One cigar to a photo, unless you are scanning a shelf or a row on purpose.
  • For a box, photograph the lid or the printed end. That is where the name, the size and the count are, and a box’s print is easier to read than a band.

Measuring the size

Lay a dollar bill or a card beside a cigar and it is measured too. The length and ring gauge are worked out against the bill or card, then matched to a size the line is actually made in. With nothing of known size in the photo, Cigar Locker does not guess the size: it lists the sizes the line is made in, and you tap yours.

The arithmetic is plain. A U.S. bill is 6.14 inches long and a credit card is 3.37 inches wide, so either gives the photo a scale. Ring gauge is a cigar’s diameter in 64ths of an inch: a 50 ring cigar is 50/64, or about 0.78 inches, across.

For the best measurement, lay the cigar flat beside the bill or card, not on top of it, and hold the phone straight above.

Why the band alone is sometimes not enough

Makers reuse bands. A line sold in a natural and a maduro wrapper often wears the same band on both, so when a line comes in more than one wrapper Cigar Locker asks which yours is instead of guessing from the color in a photo.

One band also usually covers every size in a line, which is why the size is measured, or asked about, separately. From the band alone, the wrapper and the size of such a line can only be guessed.

A shelf, a box or a row at once

A collection does not have to go in one cigar at a time. Photograph a shelf or a stack of boxes, or scan a row of loose cigars, and Cigar Locker lists the boxes and bands it can read for you to check. You give each its size and count, and they are added together.

When it is not sure

A band read with certainty is shown as “Identified as”. Anything less is shown as a “Possible match”, with close matches to choose from. You can ask it to think harder about the same photo, search the catalog yourself, or enter the cigar by hand. A name it was not sure of is asked about before it is saved.

What it does not do

  • It can be wrong, which is why you confirm every scan. A band with no lettering, or a photo taken from far away, may not be named at all.
  • We publish no accuracy figure. None has been measured on enough members’ scans for us to stand behind, and we will not print one until it has.
  • It does not tell a real cigar from a counterfeit, and it does not value or price a cigar for sale.
  • It needs a connection and a signed-in account. The photo is sent to our server and read there; the privacy policy says what is kept from it.

Questions about scanning

Can an app identify a cigar from a photo?

Often, yes. Cigar Locker reads the band or the box in your photo, compares it with the bands in its library, and names the maker and line. It tells you when it is sure and when it is only a possible match, and you confirm either way.

Does it work on a cigar with no band?

Usually not. The band and the box carry most of what identifies a cigar. Without one, Cigar Locker can still judge the shape and the wrapper’s color, but you would name the cigar yourself, by searching the catalog or typing it in.

Can it scan a whole box or a whole humidor?

Yes. Photograph a shelf or a stack of boxes, or scan a row, and it lists the boxes and bands it can read. It lists what it can read, not everything in the picture, so you check the list before adding it.

Is the cigar scanner free?

Scans come with a free allowance to start and another each month. Cigar Locker Plus, $7.99 a month in the U.S., lifts the monthly limit; a daily limit applies. Keeping your collection, humidors and journal is free.

Does it tell me whether a cigar is fake?

No. Cigar Locker identifies which cigar a band or box belongs to. It does not authenticate cigars.
